# GEI Research Methodology

## Overview

Genesis Engineered Interpretation employs a four-stage analytical methodology to examine biblical passages. Each stage builds on the previous, and an interpretation is considered supported only when multiple stages converge.

## Stage 1 — Textual Investigation

**Question: What does the passage actually say?**

- Close reading of the biblical text (KJV)
- Identification of key terms, actions, and relationships
- Mapping the sequence and structure of the narrative
- Noting what is explicitly stated versus what is inferred
- Comparing parallel passages within the scope (Genesis 1–6)

**Standard**: Observations at this stage should be verifiable by any reader of the text, regardless of interpretive framework.

## Stage 2 — Linguistic Investigation

**Question: What words, roots, and language patterns may be relevant?**

- Examination of Hebrew words and their ranges of meaning
- Identification of wordplay, homophones, homonyms, and paronomasia
- Analysis of roots and their semantic fields
- Consideration of synonyms, antonyms, and figurative language
- Cross-referencing with established Hebrew lexicons

**Standard**: Linguistic observations must distinguish between:
- **Established etymology**: Supported by standard Hebrew scholarship
- **Observed wordplay**: Phonetic or conceptual associations noted by GEI
- **Proposed connections**: Interpretive associations within the GEI framework

GEI does not claim that its proposed linguistic connections override established Hebrew definitions.

## Stage 3 — Engineering Investigation

**Question: Can the proposed interpretation be compared with an identifiable physical or engineering system?**

- Identification of a specific engineering system or process for comparison
- Point-by-point comparison between the textual description and the engineering system
- Assessment of how many points of correspondence exist
- Verification that the engineering facts cited are accurate
- Consideration of whether the parallel is specific or generic

**Standard**: Engineering facts must be independently verifiable. The comparison must be between a specific system (not a vague analogy) and the actual text (not a rewritten version of it).

## Stage 4 — Interpretive Synthesis

**Question: Do the textual, linguistic, and engineering observations converge?**

- Assessment of whether the three prior stages point in the same direction
- Identification of the strongest and weakest links in the chain
- Explicit acknowledgment of alternative interpretations
- Classification of the resulting interpretation's evidence level
- Identification of remaining research questions

**Standard**: A GEI interpretation is considered:
- **Strong** when textual, linguistic, and engineering evidence converge independently
- **Moderate** when two of three stages provide support
- **Preliminary** when only one stage provides support
- **Speculative** when the connection is proposed without substantial support from any stage

## Limitations and Integrity

- The methodology is designed to be transparent and examinable
- Every interpretation should identify its evidence classification
- The absence of evidence in one stage weakens the overall interpretation
- GEI interpretations do not invalidate other interpretive frameworks
- The methodology is a tool for investigation, not proof
