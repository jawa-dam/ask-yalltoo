# Heaven in GEI

## Biblical Context

In Genesis 1:8, God calls the firmament "Heaven" (*shamayim*). This naming associates the firmament-structure with the concept of heaven throughout the rest of the creation narrative.

## Traditional Understanding

In mainstream biblical scholarship, *shamayim* refers to the sky, the heavens, or the celestial realm. It is the domain above the earth, associated with God's dwelling, celestial bodies, and weather phenomena.

## GEI Interpretation

GEI proposes that "Heaven" in the creation narrative refers to the **upper water region** — the reservoir or water-retaining zone above the dam (firmament):

- Heaven is the **name given to the firmament** — the dam structure and its associated upper water
- It represents the elevated, water-containing portion of the hydraulic system
- When other elements are described as being "in heaven" or "in the firmament of heaven," GEI interprets this as being within or associated with the dam and reservoir system

## Linguistic Observation

The Hebrew word שָׁמַיִם (*shamayim*) contains מַיִם (*mayim*), the Hebrew word for "water."

- This is a **genuine linguistic feature** of the Hebrew word
- Standard Hebrew grammar explains *shamayim* through its dual-form construction
- GEI highlights this water connection as consistent with its interpretation but does not claim it proves the interpretation
