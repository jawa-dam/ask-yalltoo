# The Firmament — Dam Hypothesis

## Overview

The firmament (*raqia*) is described in Genesis 1:6–8 as a structure placed in the midst of the waters to divide them. GEI proposes this describes a dam — a physical barrier that separates an upper water body (reservoir) from a lower water body (downstream flow).

## The Three Functions

The text attributes three functions to the firmament:

1. **Positioned in the midst of waters** — It exists within a water environment
2. **Divides waters from waters** — It separates one body of water into two
3. **Creates above and below** — It establishes upper and lower water regions

These three functions are precisely what a dam does in a hydraulic system.

## The Name "Heaven"

The firmament is called "Heaven" (*shamayim*). GEI observes that *shamayim* (שָׁמַיִם) contains *mayim* (מַיִם, "water"). While standard Hebrew grammar explains this as a dual form meaning "heights" or "sky," GEI notes the linguistic presence of "water" within the name given to the firmament.

## Engineering Parallel

| Firmament (Text) | Dam (Engineering) |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| In the midst of the waters | Built across a water body |
| Divides waters from waters | Separates reservoir from downstream |
| Waters above | Reservoir (upstream pool) |
| Waters below | Tailwater (downstream flow) |
| Called Heaven (contains "water") | The upper water region |

- Firmament divides waters: **Textual evidence**
- Firmament is a dam: **GEI hypothesis**
- Dams divide water into upper and lower: **Established engineering fact**
