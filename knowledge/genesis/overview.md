# Genesis Engineered Interpretation — Overview

## What Is GEI?

Genesis Engineered Interpretation (GEI) is an independent research framework founded by **Wilbert Bouie Jr.** that proposes the creation narrative in Genesis chapters 1 through 6 (King James Version) can be examined through the lens of hydraulic engineering and ancient water-management systems.

GEI does not claim to replace traditional theological, literary, or historical interpretations of Genesis. Instead, it offers an alternative analytical framework that investigates whether the language, sequence, and imagery of the creation account may describe — or parallel — the construction and operation of a water-management system such as a dam, millpond, and associated hydraulic machinery.

## Core Proposition

GEI proposes that the six days of creation, as described in Genesis 1, may correspond to stages in the design, construction, and commissioning of a hydraulic engineering system:

1. **Day 1** — Separation of light and darkness: proposed separation and release of water
2. **Day 2** — The firmament: proposed dam or containment structure
3. **Day 3** — Gathering of waters and dry land: proposed millpond and managed water environment
4. **Day 4** — Sun, moon, and stars: proposed sluice gates, headrace, and water-control mechanisms
5. **Day 5** — Fish and birds (including whales): proposed waterwheels and water-powered systems
6. **Day 6** — Land creatures and humanity: proposed completed hydraulic or mill-system

## Research Methodology

GEI employs a four-stage methodology:

1. **Textual Investigation** — Close reading of the biblical text as written
2. **Linguistic Investigation** — Examination of words, roots, homophones, homonyms, and figurative language
3. **Engineering Investigation** — Comparison with identifiable physical or engineering systems
4. **Interpretive Synthesis** — Assessment of whether textual, linguistic, and engineering observations converge

## Scope

The current GEI research scope covers Genesis chapters 1 through 6 in the King James Version. The framework focuses on the six creation days and selected post-creation narratives within this range.

## Important Distinctions

- GEI interpretations are **proposed hypotheses**, not established facts
- Linguistic connections proposed by GEI are **interpretive observations**, not necessarily established Hebrew etymology
- Engineering comparisons are **analogies**, not claims of historical engineering projects
- GEI invites independent examination, challenge, and verification
