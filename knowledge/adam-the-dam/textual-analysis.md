# Adam / A-DAM — Textual Analysis

## Scope

This document examines what the Genesis text says about Adam's creation, attributes, and role — the textual foundation upon which GEI builds its interpretive hypothesis.

## Formation

**Genesis 2:7** — "And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul."

Textual observations:
- **Material**: Dust of the ground — earthen substance
- **Process**: Formed (*yatsar*) — shaped, molded, constructed
- **Activation**: Breath of life — the transition from inert material to functioning entity
- **Result**: A living soul — a functional, animate being

## Placement and Purpose

**Genesis 2:15** — "And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it."

Textual observations:
- Adam is **placed** in a specific location — installed, positioned
- Purpose is to **dress** (*abad* — to work, serve, till) the garden
- Purpose is to **keep** (*shamar* — to guard, watch, maintain) the garden
- Adam has a stewardship and maintenance function

## Authority and Naming

**Genesis 2:19–20** — Adam names the animals — an act of classification and authority.

Textual observations:
- Naming implies knowledge of the named things' nature
- Naming implies authority or governance
- Adam classifies the other created elements

## Relationship to the Ground

The text explicitly connects Adam to *adamah* (ground):
- Formed **from** the ground
- Returns **to** the ground (Genesis 3:19)
- Works **the** ground

This textual relationship between Adam and earth material is not disputed across interpretive traditions.
